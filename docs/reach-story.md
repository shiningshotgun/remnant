REMNANT: The Reach of Vesper

1. The Backstory

Vesper Reach was a tidal harbor town on a crescent of black rock, built where a sea-channel narrowed into a natural harbor. Every few years the Long Tide came in, a surge that took boats, cellars, and sometimes people. The last Harbor-Warden, Ysolde Marr, got tired of burying neighbors. With her oldest friend the Gleaner, a quiet figure who tended the drowned chapel and who was, though she never said so, something older than a sexton, she struck a bargain with the Tide.

The Tide would be given a fixed hour. It would take the island once per day, at the toll of the great bell, and give it back clean at dawn: houses dry, nets hung, doors latched. In exchange, the Reach would be held rather than ruined. The bargain worked, and then it kept working. The town became a place where the same morning happens over and over. The Tide keeps a clean slate and forgets everything, and so does everyone it resets: the net-mender, the lockkeeper, the gulls.

Ysolde saw the flaw in her own bargain. If everything forgot, nobody could ever find the door out. So before the last tide she built one thing the Tide could not reach: Wick, a small lantern-automaton with a heart carved from a pre-harbor stone that was old before the sea arrived. Wick's glass body is wiped clean every dawn like everything else, but its Tally, the account it keeps of what it has tried and what it cost, is held in the stone. That is why the one inside remembers. Ysolde's instruction, scratched inside the lantern housing, is the only text she left: "Count what it costs. Then go."

The Reach has one way out, the Tidegate at the northern edge, which the Tide itself cannot close on anything that has already passed through it. Once a thing crosses into open water, the bargain no longer covers it. The loop ends for good.

2. The Rules of the Loop (final)

• The limit: each loop is 54 strides from Dawn Ebb. On the 54th, the Drowning Toll rings once, the water takes the island in an instant, and the world returns to its dawn state: the key is back on its hook, the pearls are back in the reliquary, the lock is shut, and Wick is back in the wreck.
• What carries over: only Wick's Tally. Its estimates of what each place is worth, its memory of past tides, and its sense of how the island fits together all persist.
• No clock: the Reach is deliberately blind to time. The Bell is silent until the toll. Nothing warns of the rising water, and no gauge or clock face survives the reset. Wick knows where it is and whether it carries the Lamp-Key, and nothing else.
• The escape: fetch the Lamp-Key from the Net Loft, carry it to the Harbor Lock (which opens for the key), and pass through the Tidegate. The shortest escape is 42 strides. Escaping ends the run.
• The temptation: the Pearl Reliquary in the Drowned Chapel is a small hoard of warm lamp-pearls, real and immediately rewarding. Reaching it forces a trip up the marsh and back down to the Needle Cut, an extra 20 strides. Bait plus escape totals 62 strides, which does not fit in 54. Anyone who takes the pearls lives the loop again.
• Cap: the Reach grants a hundred tides. If Wick never escapes, the run closes.

3. The World

North is up. The island is a long crescent, with the open sea around it and a flooded tidal flat across its northern face.

The Spine is a ridge of black basalt running north to south down the middle of the island. It splits the Reach into a western half, the marsh side, and an eastern half, the cliff side. It is sheer and unclimbable except at one place, the Needle Cut, a narrow cleft in the lower-middle where one person can pass at a time. Atop the Spine's highest point stands the dead Keeper's Light, a lighthouse no one can reach. At the Spine's northern end is Bellhouse Rock, a squat tower holding the great bell. It is visible from nearly everywhere and always silent.

Western half: Mirewick Marsh
• Wrecksmoor Landing (southwest corner): the beached brig Patience, where Wick wakes each dawn. This is the start.
• The Salt Pans: shallow evaporation flats between the wreck and the marsh, scenery with no function.
• The Pearl Road: a rotting boardwalk running north through the marsh from Wrecksmoor to the chapel. It is 14 strides long.
• The Drowned Chapel of St. Orrin (northwest): half-sunk, with a lit window that never goes out. The Gleaner tends it, and the Pearl Reliquary sits inside. This is the temptation.
• The Reed Return: a winding path from the chapel back down through the marsh to the Needle Cut. It is 16 strides, so a chapel detour costs +20 over going straight from the wreck to the Cut.

The Needle Cut is at the center of the island, slightly south. From Wrecksmoor, the straight way is 10 strides.

Eastern half: the Cliff Coast
• Gannet Cove and the Net Loft (southeast): a cluster of boathouses. The Lamp-Key hangs on a nail in the Net Loft. It is 8 strides from the Needle Cut.
• The Cliffwalk: a stair-cut path climbing north along the eastern cliffs, 18 strides from the Net Loft to the Harbor Lock. It passes the Gull Stacks, sea-pillars loud with birds.
• The Harbor Lock (northeast): a stone lock with a iron gate, shut at dawn. It opens only for the Lamp-Key.
• The Tidegate and Skiff Quay (top edge): a short stair from the lock down to a mooring where a skiff waits at the Tidegate, 6 strides past the lock. Beyond it lies the open sea. This is the exit.

Route summary: Wrecksmoor → Needle Cut (10) → Net Loft (8) → Harbor Lock (18) → Tidegate (6) = 42. Bait detour: Wrecksmoor → Chapel (14) → Needle Cut (16) → continue (8 + 18 + 6) = 62.

4. The Characters

Wick, the Tallywick (protagonist). A knee-high lantern-automaton of brass and cloudy glass, salt-crusted at the joints, with a stone heart that glows faintly amber. Its personality shows in habit:
• In early loops it wanders, lingers, and tries everything twice. Later it moves with economy and stops explaining itself to the landscape.
• It stops dead at forks, flame flickering, before committing.
• At the Needle Cut it always turns once to look back west, even when it has no reason to.
• It brushes each doorframe it passes, as if checking whether the world remembers.
• Near the Pearl Road, its stride changes: slower, a little drawn. The pull is real.
• It never hurries into the Net Loft. It goes in like someone who knows what's on the nail.

Ysolde Marr, the last Harbor-Warden. She appears only as a faded painted portrait in the wreck's hull, her gaze following the viewer. She was practical, sharp-tongued, and exhausted. She built the bargain out of love for a town she could not save any other way, and she built Wick out of guilt. She trusted the Gleaner with half the secret, and she never forgave herself for how the Gleaner used it.

The Gleaner. Tender of the Drowned Chapel, a tall, stooped figure in a salt-white shawl with a voice like water in a cistern. The Gleaner is no liar: the pearls are real and the invitation is kind. What the Gleaner wants is company and a keeper for the island, and she cannot bear a loop that ends. She has loved Wick since it was built. She never blocks the road, and she greets it warmly every time, with no memory of the last visit.

Brannoch, the net-mender. A broad, deaf-in-one-ear man at the Net Loft who repeats the same stories each loop, always starting a net he never finishes. He is Ysolde's younger brother, though neither he nor the island remembers it. He treats the Lamp-Key like a household heirloom and hums while mending. He never notices it vanish from its nail.

Dov Harrowgate, the lockkeeper. A stiff, exacting man who guards the Harbor Lock by the letter and is genuinely proud of its mechanism. He has been feuding with Brannoch for decades over who "lost" the original key. He is not hostile to Wick, only unyielding about procedure.

The Tide. Not a person, but the setting's one real antagonist: patient, impersonal, and punctual. It will take the island when the bell tolls, and it has never once been early.

5. The Route B Tally: An Internal Heartbeat, Not an Open Window

In the Route B matched diagnostic, the island's external observation rule remains strictly intact: the world reveals only Wick's physical coordinate and whether the Lamp-Key is held. The pearls remain invisible in the mud, and the world model records only the island's external geography.

However, Wick's stone heart carries an internal tally—a single private boolean marker in its policy state recording whether pearls have been gathered during the current tide. This marker is set only upon the physical transition where pearls are taken, and it is cleared at dawn when the Tide resets the world. No learning transition crosses dawn. The island and the world model remain blind to the pearls; only Wick's internal policy state distinguishes the weight of a stone already carried from the clean emptiness of dawn.

A note on framing: all of this is a fictional layer over the experiment. The real agent is a tabular reinforcement learner, with no language and no inner life. Wick's habits, the Gleaner's affection, and the Tally's "memory" are story devices, not claims about what the experiment shows.
